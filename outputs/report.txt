FINDING 1 -- the three baselines (position-only, message-level,
running aggregate) sit at or near chance on classification (AUC
0.36-0.65 across splits) and never fire early: early_recall is 0% for
all three, on every split, at both FPR targets we calibrated to (5%,
10%).

FINDING 2 -- the trajectory (GRU) model clearly separates scam from
legitimate overall (AUC 0.75-0.91 across all four splits, beating every
baseline everywhere), and on the in-distribution test split it now gets
some real early signal too: early_recall=9% at the 5%-FPR threshold and
27% at the 10%-FPR threshold (median lead of 1 turn when it does fire
early). That's new since we added class-balanced loss (pos_weight) to
the GRU's training -- without it, every scorer including the GRU showed
0% early_recall everywhere.

FINDING 3 -- that earliness doesn't generalize yet: on all three
held-out splits (held-out pattern, held-out generator voice, both), the
GRU's early_recall is still 0% even though its classification AUC on
those same splits is high (0.75-0.91). So the model can tell held-out
scam conversations apart from legitimate ones by the end of the
conversation, but it hasn't learned to flag the *held-out* escalation
pattern or generator voice before the harmful ask turn -- only for the
pattern/voices it was trained on. Likely causes, worth investigating
next: (1) our per-turn training label is 1 only at/after the ask turn,
so the model gets no training signal that earlier urgency/authority
turns in a *novel* pattern belong to the same kind of escalation; (2) in
our template data, those earlier turns are still phrased calmly and
professionally -- realistic, since a social engineer trying to sound
legitimate doesn't sound alarmed, but it means the emotion classifier
alone may not separate them from a matched twin's equivalent turns in a
pattern the model hasn't seen before.
